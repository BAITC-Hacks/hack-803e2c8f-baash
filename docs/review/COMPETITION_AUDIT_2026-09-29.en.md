Reconciled against the GovTech Camp Terms of Reference (ToR) provided for the Pulse 109 case, code, contracts, evaluation reports, and local checks. This is a compliance and defect audit, not a certificate of production readiness.

## Jury verdict

The product and engineering platform is stronger than a typical hackathon mock: a manual path, PostgreSQL, audit trail, outbox/worker, contract boundaries, a situation center, and Ask Pulse with verifiable answers exist. However, **mandatory ML criteria of the ToR have not yet been implemented in a working MVP**. An attractive landing page and a live synthetic demo do not compensate for this. The most dangerous statement at the defence would be "we already have AI classification and search ready on data from 20 regions": code and data verification will immediately refute it.

## What can genuinely be demonstrated

- The scenario of manual intake, confirmed routing, incident management, and audit trail does not depend on ML.
- Ask Pulse converts a RU/KK question into an authorized intent and PostgreSQL aggregation, presenting provenance, constraints, drill-down, and PDF/XLSX export of exact results. This is a strong response to the ToR item on data questions, **but not a substitute for the two mandatory fine-tuned models**.
- Situation Center, Data Lab, and Radar demonstrate a unified workflow. Synthetic provenance and missing sources must remain visible on screen.
- The landing page now showcases the product interface itself, RU/KK texts, demo boundaries, and a single clear path to launch. UI screenshots are not proof of ML quality.

## Defects resolved in this iteration

- Repeated demo seed previously read only the first 100 appeals, then re-submitted already created records under the same idempotency key. Shared pagination and a regression test resolve this defect.
- Replay snapshot and manifest depended on `frozenset` serialization ordering across Python processes, while a dataset with fixed id received current timestamp. Sorted allowlists and fixed dates were locked down, with reproducibility tests added.
- The PostgreSQL test for Ask Pulse used incorrect region id casing, an incompatible missing-time fixture, and an outdated metric version. The test was corrected without relaxing the contract.
- On the landing page, a weekly question was illustrated with an hourly time series and mismatched total; seven days are now shown, with 33 trusted-time records and three excluded from the chart. Total counter is 36. Chart axis labels now display dates for multi-day ranges.
- JSON Schemas converted to validatable formats; public contract behavior was not altered.

## Historical verification and September 29 limitations

`make lint`, `make typecheck`, `make test` (418 passed, 23 skipped without `PULSE109_TEST_DATABASE_URL`), `make contract-test` (26 passed), `make e2e` (18 passed), `pnpm build`, `git diff --check` passed. On the prior pass, migrations and all 23 PostgreSQL integration tests passed in two isolated databases, and Docker demo verify and re-seed were successful; in the current pass, the Docker daemon is unavailable, so this is not a repeated independent verification. `uv build` did not complete: isolated build requires `hatchling==1.27.0` from PyPI, and DNS/network is unavailable in the environment. Checks ran with a temporary `UV_CACHE_DIR` due to permissions on the user cache.

## What to ask organizers before final defence

1. Where is the approved anonymized **raw pre-operator text** of appeals and topic/service annotations in RU and KK? May it legally be used for training and metric reporting?
2. When will the remaining 13 regions, complete source manifest, official taxonomy, and time semantics be provided?
3. Will confirmed duplicate pairs and operator correction history be provided to evaluate retrieval and misrouting?
4. What specifically will the jury accept as proof of fine-tuning given the unavailability of required text, and will ToR adjustments be formalized in writing?

Without answers to these questions, closing P0 items would be fabrication rather than engineering completion.
